# Harmony Wheel and Calipers

The Harmony Wheel adapts the **Circle of Fifths** into a visual songwriting workspace. It is not a notation staff or a measure of time.

## Tonal center and mode

Choose the key around which you want to explore. The wheel's outer ring presents major tonal centers and its inner ring presents related minor harmony. Actual suggestions depend on the current key and harmony settings.

## Calipers and relationships

Caliper endpoints select two harmonic targets. A connecting line or arc represents the chosen relationship. **Caliper span** describes the selected wheel relationship, not a Roman-numeral chord function.

For instance, moving by fifths can emphasize dominant motion; major-third and minor-third relationships can be used in more symmetrical or color-based suggestions.

## Relationship Overlay

Available relationship lenses include:

- Circle of Fifths and Fifth Movement
- Major Thirds and Minor Thirds
- Chromatic Neighbors
- Modal Borrowing
- Symmetrical Harmony

These are **ways to explore**, not guarantees that every suggested progression follows traditional functional harmony.

## Selected progression path

Select a **Suggested Progression** to update the wheel's path. When you're browsing below the wheel, tap **View on Wheel** to inspect the visual relationship. The progression title in the selected preview can link back to its source card.

Depending on display controls, you can focus on the relationship, progression path, or both. Chords and Roman numerals are explained in [Progressions](progressions.md).

## What the wheel does not mean

- A colored wedge is not a standard notation pitch.
- The visual span is not the Roman-numeral function.
- Caliper-derived suggestions are creative relationships, not necessarily verified cadences.
