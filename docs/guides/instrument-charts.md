# Piano and Fretted Instrument Charts

Instrument View shows chord information for a selected chord or steps through a progression.

## Piano

Piano offers three display modes:

- **Keyboard**: highlighted keys for a piano chord/voicing.
- **Staff**: bass and treble grand-staff notation for the represented piano voicing.
- **Both**: staff and keyboard shown together.

The voicing panel separates **Bass**, **Treble**, and **Chord tones**. A chord should be drawn as a **simultaneous vertical sonority** on the staff, not as a left-to-right melody. Sharps and flats use notation accidentals such as ♯ and ♭; bass notes may need ledger lines.

The default Piano display is Keyboard. The active Piano display mode also determines corresponding piano-chart export modes when those exports are selected.

## Fretted instruments

Supported views include:

- Guitar (six strings)
- Mandolin (four courses)
- Mandola (four courses)
- Banjo (five-string arrangement)
- Ukulele (four strings)
- Baritone Ukulele (four strings)

The correct chord shape depends on **instrument tuning**. The same fret pattern can produce different notes on mandolin and mandola, or standard and baritone ukulele. Never assume diagrams are interchangeable.

## Reading fretted charts

An **X** marks a muted string, **O** an open string, and a filled dot a fretted position. A label such as **3fr** indicates the diagram begins above the first fret. Confirm the string labels and sounding chord before playing unfamiliar shapes.

## Chart availability

Some uncommon chords may use a partial, rootless, or first-pass voicing, or may display a fallback instead of an inaccurate diagram. The app's helper text indicates when a shape needs confirmation by ear.

## Exports

See [Exporting Images and PDF](exports.md). Piano can use Keyboard, Staff, or Both. When Both needs more room, exports can span pages instead of hiding chords.
